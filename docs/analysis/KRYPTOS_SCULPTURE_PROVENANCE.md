# Kryptos sculpture: creator, fabrication, and archival provenance

> 🧭 [kryptos](../../README.md) · [Docs index](../INDEX.md) · [K4 negative space](K4_NEGATIVE_SPACE.md) · [World Clock source audit](K4_WORLD_CLOCK_HISTORICAL_SOURCES.md) <!-- nav -->

**Status:** sourced provenance note; not a complete fabrication roster or conservation file.  
**Updated:** 2026-10-22

## Short answer

**Jim Sanborn (James Sanborn) is the credited artist and creator of *Kryptos*.** The CIA and the U.S. General Services Administration (GSA) independently identify Sanborn as the artist; GSA records the work as a 1990 commission through its Art in Architecture Program. The work was dedicated on 3 November 1990.

That does not mean Sanborn fabricated every component alone. Reporting on the construction says Sanborn worked in his Washington, D.C. studio with multiple assistants who cut the copper letters. A 2025 *Financial Times* account identifies sculptor **David Sheldon** as an expert cutter who became Sanborn's right hand during construction and installation. This is a secondary-source identification; the official CIA/GSA catalogue pages do not provide a full fabrication crew or contractor list. No source found in this pass establishes a separate outside company as the sole fabricator.

**Edward M. Scheidt had a different role:** the retired CIA cryptographer collaborated with Sanborn on the cryptographic systems. He should not be described as the sculptor or physical fabricator. The text's literary collaboration is another separate role and is not identified conclusively in the official catalogue pages cited below.

## Role distinctions

| Role | Person / organization | What the sources support |
|---|---|---|
| Credited artist / creator | Jim (James) Sanborn | CIA and GSA both credit Sanborn with the artwork. |
| Physical construction | Sanborn and workshop assistants | Contemporary/historical accounts describe Sanborn's hands-on construction and assistants cutting the copper letters. |
| Named fabrication assistant | David Sheldon | A 2025 *Financial Times* account identifies Sheldon as an expert cutter and close assistant during construction and installation. Confirm against primary records before treating this as a complete crew list. |
| Cryptographic collaborator | Edward M. Scheidt | A retired CIA cryptographer who worked with Sanborn to devise the cipher systems; not the credited sculptor. |
| Commissioning / public-art administration | U.S. General Services Administration, Art in Architecture Program; CIA Fine Arts Commission participated in selection | GSA catalogue records the commission through Art in Architecture; CIA history describes the joint selection process. |
| Archive / primary documentation | Archives of American Art, Smithsonian Institution | The Jim Sanborn papers include commission files, contracts, designs/sketches, budgets, correspondence, notes, and multiple *Kryptos*-specific folders. This is the best located archival route for a more precise fabrication record. |

## Primary sources and archive leads

1. **CIA, “Kryptos” sculpture history:** https://www.cia.gov/legacy/headquarters/kryptos-sculpture/  
   Identifies James Sanborn's concept, the 1988 selection, the 3 November 1990 dedication, physical components and materials, and the cryptographic collaboration with a retired CIA cryptographer.

2. **GSA Fine Arts Collection, “Kryptos”:** https://art.gsa.gov/artworks/23717/kryptos  
   Credits “Artist Jim Sanborn,” dates the work to 1990, and records the commission through the GSA Art in Architecture Program.

3. **Smithsonian Archives of American Art, Jim Sanborn papers finding aid:** https://www.aaa.si.edu/collections/jim-sanborn-papers-22298  
   The collection's commission files contain project applications, contracts, designs and sketches, budgets, photographs, correspondence, and notes. The finding aid lists *Kryptos* sculpture materials in Box 6, Folder 8; *Kryptos* pre-production and notes in Box 6, Folder 10; dedication material in Box 6, Folder 12; and other code/research folders. See also the folder record: https://www.aaa.si.edu/collections/jim-sanborn-papers-22298/series-9/box-16-folder-2. The Smithsonian is the strongest next step for identifying contracts, shop records, assistants, suppliers, or installation credits.

4. **Smithsonian oral history interview with Jim Sanborn (14–16 July 2009):** https://www.aaa.si.edu/collections/interviews/oral-history-interview-jim-sanborn-15700  
   Useful first-person source for Sanborn's fabrication practices and working methods. It discusses his later water-jet cutting vendor for other works, but that statement alone does **not** identify the fabricator of the original 1990 *Kryptos* copper screen.

5. **The *Financial Times*, “Jim Sanborn has a secret” (2025; archived copy):** https://archive.is/2025.08.16-065226/https%3A/www.ft.com/content/552635c5-d2e3-4d32-9e14-982040a5c710  
   Reports that construction took place in a warehouse studio in northeast Washington, D.C., involved multiple assistants cutting thick copper, and identifies David Sheldon as an expert cutter and close assistant through construction and installation. Treat this as a lead to verify against Sanborn's papers, not as an official complete fabrication roster.

6. **CIA Museum artifact record:** https://www.cia.gov/legacy/museum/artifact/kryptos/  
   Confirms Sanborn as the artist and describes the copper screen, granite elements, pool, and petrified wood.

## Follow-up questions for the Smithsonian archive

Request the *Kryptos* commission and pre-production files, especially:
- executed GSA commission/contract and any fabrication or installation subcontract;
- design drawings, shop drawings, material invoices, and studio photographs;
- names/roles of assistants and any metalworking or stonework vendors;
- copper plate cutting, assembly, transport, and installation records;
- any artist's statement or production notes distinguishing Sanborn's own work from assistant/vendor work.

Do not infer that a named supplier for Sanborn's later water-jet-cut works fabricated the original *Kryptos*. The sources currently establish **Sanborn as creator and hands-on workshop lead, with assistants including David Sheldon reported by a secondary source**; they do not yet establish a complete production roster or a sole outside fabricator.
